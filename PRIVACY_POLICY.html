Privacy Policy

Operated independently by Starfall

Effective September 4, 2026 · Version 1.8

A Discord and VRChat bot. An independent project - not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Discord or VRChat.

In short

  • YORI is a moderation tool. It logs the actions staff take against VRChat group members - warnings, mutes, kicks, bans, and group-role changes - and supports moderation on the linked Discord server too.
  • It keeps a running count of how many messages each Discord member sends (a number, not what the messages say), and it fingerprints avatar images (a short mathematical hash, never a copy of the picture) to compare them against a staff watch-list.
  • When someone joins the VRChat group, YORI checks their public profile against a staff-configured watch-list and, if something matches, flags it for a human to review - it records what matched, not a copy of your profile.
  • It does not keep the text of your chat messages (one narrow exception, described below), your DMs, or any payment or real-world identity information.
  • A human makes nearly every enforcement decision. YORI's checks normally only raise alerts. The exception is optional, off-by-default anti-spam enforcement that can apply a temporary, reversible Discord timeout (1 hour by default) on its own when it detects spam, a blocked image, or a flood of slur or hate terms; staff are notified and can undo it in a click.
  • Data lives in a private database run by the bot's operator (Starfall), with encrypted backups. It is never sold or shared with advertisers. You can ask staff to see, correct, or delete data about you - see Your rights.

1Scope

This policy describes how the YORI Moderation Bot ("YORI," "the bot," "we," "us") collects, uses, stores, and protects data while helping volunteer staff moderate The Virtual Reality Show (TVRS), a VRChat group and its associated Discord server. It applies to three groups of people: staff members who run the bot's commands, VRChat group members whose accounts are moderated, and Discord server members whose accounts are moderated on the Discord side.

YORI is operated independently by Starfall, not by Discord Inc. or VRChat Inc. Your use of Discord and VRChat themselves is separately governed by Discord's own privacy policy and VRChat's own privacy policy.

2What we collect

From VRChat group members being moderated

  • VRChat user ID and display name, looked up automatically when a log entry is created or a member is checked.
  • The rule that was broken, the action taken (warn, mute, kick, ban, unban, or a group-role change), a written description, any staff notes, the staff member who logged it, and the time.
  • VRChat group role and membership status, checked live when a staff member looks a member up; role changes are recorded in the log. When a member is banned, YORI additionally records whether they were a member of each of the group(s) affected at that moment - a single yes/no per group - so that if the ban is later lifted, staff can offer to re-invite them to the group(s) they were in. This indicator is kept only while the ban is in place: it is cleared automatically when the ban is lifted, and it is also removed if the member's data is erased.
  • Avatar image fingerprint (checked, not stored). When a member joins or is checked, YORI computes a short mathematical fingerprint of their avatar image, never a copy of the image, and compares it against a staff-maintained watch-list. On the VRChat side this fingerprint is used for the comparison and then discarded; it is not kept.
  • Join-time profile screening. When someone joins the VRChat group, YORI checks their public profile against a staff-configured watch-list. If it matches, YORI posts a note to a staff channel and creates an internal moderation record indicating what matched - not a copy of the member's profile text - and raises a ban request for a staff member to approve or dismiss. It does not ban anyone automatically.
  • Event / hangout records. When staff log a group hangout, YORI records who hosted or co-hosted it, the activity type, and any notes (via the /event commands).
  • Group invitations. When a staff member invites someone to one of the VRChat group(s) YORI manages (for example, onboarding a new staff member, or re-inviting a member whose ban was lifted), YORI keeps a short accountability record of who was invited, to which group, which staff member sent it, and when. It keeps no display name or profile information about the invited person, only their VRChat ID, and the record is removed if the member's data is erased.

From Discord server members being moderated

  • Discord user ID, username, global display name, and an avatar key (an identifier for the member's current avatar image).
  • An avatar image fingerprint, cached per member so the same unchanged avatar isn't re-checked repeatedly. As above, this is a short mathematical fingerprint, never a copy of the image, and it is replaced when the member changes their avatar.
  • A running count of how many messages each member has sent since counting began for that server - the count only, not the text or content of any message.
  • Message content, read in real time to detect spam. YORI checks for patterns typical of spam. It processes the text in memory only and does not save it to its database. When a spam alert is raised and staff have turned the option on, a short, truncated excerpt of the flagged message is shown in a staff-only channel so staff can see what was flagged; the rest is reached by a link. Separately, if staff enable the optional auto-delete mode (off by default), then when YORI removes a caught spam message it keeps a full copy of that message's text in a staff-only channel, as a record of what was removed and why. YORI does not read direct messages.
  • Separate records per platform. YORI keeps VRChat and Discord moderation records separately and does not automatically link a person's VRChat and Discord identities; each moderation record belongs to one platform. Staff may note an identity from the other platform in a free-text field. Because there is no automatic link, a deletion request that names only one platform's ID reaches only that platform's data - give staff both IDs if you want records on both cleared.

From staff members

  • Discord user ID and username, recorded against every action they log and every ban request they raise, so entries have a clear, accountable author.
  • Which of the bot's commands they use and how often, kept as aggregate counts for internal usage statistics - not message content.
  • A record of any change they make to the bot's staff permission roles - who made the change, when, and which permission level was changed - so that changes to who holds moderation powers are accountable and reviewable. This applies only to the small number of staff able to change those settings.

What YORI does not collect

  • The text of your chat messages is not stored in YORI's database, and YORI never reads your direct messages. (YORI does read message content in memory to detect spam, and may show staff a short excerpt of a flagged message; and if the optional auto-delete mode is enabled, it keeps a full copy of a deleted spam message in a staff-only channel - not in its database, but a durable copy nonetheless. See "From Discord server members being moderated" above.)
  • Your direct messages (DMs).
  • Payment, real-world identity, or contact information of any kind.
  • VRChat browsing activity, friend lists, or anything beyond the profile and membership fields described above.

3Why we process this data

We rely on legitimate interest as the legal basis for processing (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)): keeping a clear, accountable record of moderation decisions is necessary to run a safe community, resolve disputes fairly, and prevent repeat rule-breaking. A person who is banned or warned has not "consented" to being logged in the ordinary sense - the record exists because the community has a legitimate need to document why an enforcement action was taken, proportionate to that purpose and no more.

Some profile fields screened at join time - notably pronouns - can reveal or imply gender identity. Because the community YORI serves is centred on trans and gender-diverse members, this information is handled with particular care: it is used only to help staff keep the space safe, never to single anyone out for who they are.

For staff, usage is processed as part of operating the community's own moderation tooling - the same basis, applied to the people running it rather than the people it is used on.

4Storage & security

All moderation data lives in a database file on a virtual machine hosted with a cloud infrastructure provider. That machine is administered exclusively by the bot's operator (Starfall) - the cloud provider supplies the underlying hardware and network only, has no moderation-tool access of its own, and is not accessible to Discord or VRChat themselves. Community staff run the bot's commands; they do not administer the server or the database.

  • Access to every command is gated by Discord role, checked fresh on every use - someone without the right role gets a private "you don't have permission" reply, nothing more.
  • Backups are taken on a schedule and checked for integrity; a corrupted backup is quarantined automatically rather than silently trusted.
  • Off-site backups are encrypted before they leave the server, so the off-site copy is unreadable ciphertext to anyone who holds it (see Sharing & third parties).
  • The bot's own service-account credentials (used solely to act on VRChat, e.g. to execute a ban) are stored in a restricted configuration file, never logged, and never shown in any Discord message.
  • When something goes wrong internally, the technical detail goes to a private error-reporting channel and the bot's own logs - never to the person who ran the command.

Deletion is real Once a log entry is permanently deleted by staff, it is gone from the database and from every backup taken after that point - there is no hidden retention beyond what is described above. One caveat: some actions also post a notice into a staff-only Discord channel (for example an alert, an audit notice, or a ban-request or invite result). Those channel messages are not part of the database; when a member's data is erased, staff remove any such messages by a separate manual sweep rather than by the database deletion, and Discord's own copy follows Discord's retention, not YORI's.

6Who can access it

Access is split into cumulative staff tiers, each gated by a Discord role - nobody outside these roles can query the bot for moderation data:

  • Lead Log new actions and view active history for a specific person.
  • Senior Lead The above, plus executing live VRChat bans and unbans.
  • Auditor The above, plus reviewing and restoring removed entries, and backup management.
  • Head Lead Full access, including permanent deletion, database restore, and bot configuration.

A separate Disc Mod tier gates the Discord-platform commands (such as Discord member checks and the Discord avatar watch-list). A Head Lead can use every command regardless of tier.

7Sharing & third parties

We do not sell data, and we do not share it with advertisers or data brokers. Data leaves our database only where it is operationally necessary:

  • To Discord - when a log entry, report, or flag is posted as a message in a channel your staff configured for that purpose.
  • To VRChat - when a command actually executes an action there, such as a ban, unban, or group-role change.
  • To our cloud hosting provider - which stores the underlying virtual machine and disk as an infrastructure sub-processor. It has no operational access to the bot, Discord, or VRChat, and is bound by its own hosting-provider security commitments.
  • To GitHub - encrypted database backups are pushed off-site to a private GitHub repository, where GitHub acts as a storage sub-processor. Backups are encrypted before they leave the server; GitHub stores ciphertext it cannot read.

8Your rights

You can ask community staff to:

  • See what is logged about you (access).
  • Correct inaccurate entries (rectification).
  • Delete data about you (erasure) - subject to a legitimate need to keep a record of past enforcement actions.

We handle these requests manually, through staff. We do not currently offer a one-click, automated data export; if you ask for a copy of your data, we will provide what we reasonably can, in a readable form.

If you are located in the European Economic Area, the UK, or another jurisdiction with similar protections, you also have rights to restrict or object to processing carried out under legitimate interest, and to data portability where technically applicable. We act on these case by case - contact staff and we will respond.

If you are a California resident, you have rights under the CCPA/CPRA to know what personal information is collected and disclosed, to delete it (subject to the same enforcement-record exception above), to opt out of any sale or sharing (moot here - we do neither), and to non-discrimination for exercising these rights.

How to exercise these rights Contact staff as described in Contact us. We will respond within a reasonable time and may need to verify your identity before acting on a request.

9Automated actions

A human carries out or approves nearly every enforcement action. The exception, described below, is an optional anti-spam mode that is off by default; apart from it, YORI's automated parts only surface things for a person to review.

Most of YORI's detection is automated, but only surfaces things for a person to review:

  • It flags new members whose public profile matches a staff watch-list, and - for a match - creates an internal moderation record and a ban request that a staff member must approve or dismiss. The request is never carried out automatically.
  • It compares avatar fingerprints against a watch-list and posts an advisory alert on a match.
  • It can alert staff to possible coordinated join activity for them to review.
  • It detects likely spam or coordinated abuse in Discord messages and posts an advisory alert with manual action buttons.

Automatic anti-spam enforcement is an optional mode, off by default. When staff enable it, YORI can apply a temporary Discord timeout (1 hour by default, set by staff) to a member on its own, without a staff member approving that specific action, when it detects spam or abusive content - for example a burst of spam messages, a blocked image, or a flood of slurs or hate terms. When the optional auto-delete setting is also on, YORI additionally removes the offending messages on its own. Wider optional settings, all off by default, let it act on a broader set of those signals. An automatic timeout can fall on a member who has done nothing wrong, and the wider automatic settings carry a higher chance of that.

Automatic timeouts are temporary and fully logged. A staff member is notified and can lift a timeout with one click, which removes it from your active moderation history; a hidden record of the action is kept until a Head Lead permanently deletes it. YORI does not send you a separate notice - Discord itself shows that you have been timed out. If auto-delete removed any of your messages, lifting the timeout does not restore them. To dispute an automatic timeout, contact a staff member (for example in the server's mod-mail or support channel). Every other enforcement outcome is decided by a human.

10Children's privacy

Both Discord and VRChat require account holders to meet their own platforms' minimum age requirements. We do not knowingly direct this bot at, or collect data specifically about, children below those thresholds beyond what those platforms already permit.
